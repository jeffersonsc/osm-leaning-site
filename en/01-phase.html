What is phase

time delay measured in degrees,
frequency by frequency

It's not level, it's time.

Phase is the time delay between the signal that left the mixer and the signal that reached the microphone — measured in degrees, frequency by frequency.

Why degrees? Because absolute time (ms) doesn't say much: 1 ms completely shifts a 1000 Hz wave but only half a wave at 500 Hz. Degrees normalize this by frequency: 360° = one full wave, regardless of frequency.

Rule of thumb

360° of delay = one full cycle of that frequency.

180° = half a wave — one speaker pushes "up" while the other pushes "down".

Two waves at the same frequency
How it shows in OSM (phase graph)
✓ In practice, phase matters when:

Two speakers play the same frequency — sub + top, main + rear fill, stereo L + R at a center point.

If phases are close, speakers sum. If they're opposite, they cancel.

Magnitude alone lies: one speaker's curve can be perfect, but at the point where two overlap, phase decides whether it'll work.
